About

A physicist learning to ask better questions of data

Physics graduate from the University of Ruhuna, CERN Summer Student alumnus, and temporary university lecturer — preparing for graduate research at the intersection of physics and machine learning.

Background

I am a Sri Lankan physicist and early-career researcher. I completed my BSc Honours in Physics at the University of Ruhuna, where my final-year research project was an observational astronomy study: astrometric measurements and analysis of the visual binary star STF 1434, using images from the Las Cumbres Observatory global telescope network.

I was then selected for the CERN Summer Student Programme, one of the most competitive international research internships in physics. Working with the ATLAS Collaboration, I studied machine-learning approaches to Higgs boson mass reconstruction in tHq production with H → ττ decays — building neural-network regression models on event-level data with Python, TensorFlow/Keras, and ROOT/C++.

Teaching

Alongside research, I have been working as a Temporary Assistant Lecturer in Physics, supporting undergraduate courses in electricity and magnetism, computational physics,thermodynamics, tutorials, and examination preparation. Teaching has sharpened how I explain ideas — a skill I consider inseparable from doing good science.

Where I'm headed

My goal is to pursue graduate research (MSc/PhD) in particle physics, astrophysics, AI for science, or quantum technologies. I am particularly drawn to problems where careful machine learning can extend the reach of experiments — from reconstructing invisible particles at the LHC to classifying objects in astronomical surveys — and I am actively seeking scholarship and research opportunities in these areas.
