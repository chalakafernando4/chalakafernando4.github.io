BSc Honours Research · University of Ruhuna

Astrometric Measurements and Analysis of STF 1434 Double Star

2023-2024 · Completed

AstronomyAstrometryPhotometry

Overview

For my final-year research project in the BSc Honours programme at the University of Ruhuna, I carried out an observational astronomy study of STF 1434, a visual binary star system from the Washington double star catalogue. Using images obtained through the Las Cumbres Observatory (LCO) global telescope network, I measured the system’s key astrometric and photometric properties.

Motivation

Double Stars are one of astronomy’s most direct laboratories: tracking the relative positions of the two components over time constrains their orbit, and ultimately the stellar masses — one of the few ways masses can be measured directly. Measurements of double stars by small-telescope observers also contribute genuinely useful data points to long-baseline orbital records. This project developed the full observational workflow: from proposing and scheduling observations to extracting calibrated measurements from the images.

Methods

  • Target selection: evaluated candidate double stars for observability (separation, magnitudes, sky position) and selected STF 1434.
  • Observations: acquired CCD images of the system through the LCO robotic telescope network.
  • Image analysis & astrometry: identified the primary and secondary components and measured their relative positions on calibrated images.
  • Position angle and separation: determined the position angle (θ) and angular separation (ρ) of the pair.
  • Aperture photometry: measured the brightness of each component and computed the magnitude difference (Δm).
  • Compared the results against historical catalogue measurements of the system.

Results & outputs

The project produced measured values of the position angle, angular separation, and magnitude difference of STF 1434, documented in my BSc thesis.
