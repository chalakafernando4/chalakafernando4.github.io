Quantum Machine Learning and AI for Physics

Ongoing · Research direction

Quantum ComputingMachine LearningParticle Physics

Overview

This page describes research directions I am preparing to pursue in graduate study — not completed projects or publications. It reflects where my CERN/ATLAS experience, astronomy research, and machine-learning projects are pointing: toward quantum machine learning (QML) and AI methods for fundamental physics.

Why these directions

My summer-student work on Higgs mass reconstruction showed me both the power and the limits of classical ML in particle physics: performance is often bounded by how well we can represent combinatorially hard parts of the event, and by how much training data and compute we can use. Two directions follow naturally:

Quantum machine learning for physics data

Quantum computing offers fundamentally different ways to represent and process correlations. I am interested in how near-term QML models — such as variational quantum circuits — might be applied to classification and regression problems on particle-physics data, and in the honest question that comes with the hype: where, if anywhere, do quantum models offer a real advantage over classical baselines?

ML for event reconstruction: lepton assignment and beyond

In multi-lepton final states (like the tHq channel I worked on), a central combinatorial problem is assigning reconstructed leptons and jets to their parent particles. I want to explore modern architectures — including attention-based and graph-based networks — for assignment, classification, and regression tasks at the event level, with careful treatment of systematic uncertainties.

Scientific machine learning

More broadly, I’m drawn to scientific ML: models that respect physical constraints (symmetries, conservation laws), uncertainty-aware predictions, and reproducible pipelines that other researchers can build on.

Current preparation

  • Strengthening foundations in quantum information and quantum computing.
  • Continuing hands-on ML practice through the projects on this site.
  • Following the QML-for-HEP literature and community efforts.

What I’m looking for

I am seeking MSc/PhD positions and research collaborations where these directions can be developed rigorously — in particle physics groups using advanced ML, quantum computing groups interested in scientific applications, or interdisciplinary AI-for-science programmes. If that sounds like your group, please get in touch.
