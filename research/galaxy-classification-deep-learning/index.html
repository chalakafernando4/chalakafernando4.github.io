Group Project · AI for Astronomy

Galaxy Classification Using Deep Learning

2025 - Present · Ongoing

Deep LearningAstronomyComputer Vision

Overview

A deep-learning project applying convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to the classification of galaxy morphologies from astronomical survey images — distinguishing classes such as spiral and elliptical galaxies directly from image data.

Motivation

Modern sky surveys image far more galaxies than humans can ever inspect: upcoming facilities will produce catalogues of billions of objects. Morphological classification — historically done by eye — is exactly the kind of task deep learning handles well, and it matters scientifically because morphology traces galaxy formation and evolution. This project was my hands-on exploration of AI for astronomy, connecting my physics background with computer-vision methods.

Methods

  • Used a public galaxy-morphology dataset of labelled survey images.
  • Preprocessed images: resizing, normalisation, and data augmentation (rotations/flips — natural augmentations for galaxies, which have no preferred orientation).
  • Designed and trained CNN classifiers in TensorFlow/Keras.
  • Evaluated performance with accuracy and per-class metrics, and inspected misclassified examples to understand failure modes.

Results & outputs

The trained CNN classifies galaxy morphologies from raw images, with performance summarised by accuracy and a confusion matrix. The results and outputs will be updated as soon as the project completed.

Results (accuracy, confusion matrix, example classifications) and the notebook link will be added here.

My contribution

I built the full workflow: dataset preparation, augmentation strategy, CNN architecture, training, and evaluation, including the analysis of misclassified galaxies.

Skills demonstrated

  • Computer vision with CNNs: architecture design, augmentation, regularisation.
  • Working with astronomical image datasets.
  • Model evaluation beyond a single number: per-class behaviour and failure analysis.
  • Scientific framing of an ML problem — connecting morphology to the underlying astrophysics.
