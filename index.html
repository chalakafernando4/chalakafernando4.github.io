Sri Lankan Physicist · Early-Career Researcher

Chalaka Fernando

Physics graduate, CERN Summer Student, temporary university lecturer, and aspiring researcher working at the intersection of physics, machine learning, and data analysis.

I hold a BSc Honours in Physics from the University of Ruhuna, Sri Lanka, and was selected for the CERN Summer Student Programme, where I worked with the ATLAS Collaboration on machine-learning methods for Higgs boson mass reconstruction. My interests span particle physics, astrophysics, quantum computing, and AI for science — and I am preparing for graduate research (MSc/PhD) in these fields.

Research Mission

Why I do physics

The questions I care about — how the Higgs boson couples to matter, how stars and galaxies evolve, how quantum systems can be harnessed for computation — all share one feature: the data has outgrown the human eye. My mission is to build careful, transparent machine-learning and computational tools that help physicists extract more from their experiments, and to do so in a way that is reproducible and honest about uncertainty.

Coming from Sri Lanka, I also care about widening the path I took: showing students from smaller research communities that contributing to experiments like ATLAS, and to the future of AI-driven science, is within reach.
